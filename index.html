Can Nebraska's Retooled Offensive Line Finally Solve Its Trench Problem?

A portal-built unit with 100-plus starts of experience faces its first real test — and the results so far are encouraging, if unproven.

33
SACKS ALLOWED IN 2025 — TIED FOR 115TH NATIONALLY

For years, Nebraska's offensive line has been the punchline of the program's struggles. In 2025, the Huskers allowed 33 sacks, tying for 115th nationally — not the kind of number that wins games in the Big Ten. Heading into 2026, fixing the trenches became priority one.

A Complete Overhaul

Matt Rhule brought in a new voice to lead the room: offensive line coach Geep Wade, hired after a strong run at Georgia Tech, paired with run-game coordinator Lonnie Teasley from South Carolina. Together they rebuilt the position almost entirely through the transfer portal, adding three projected starters. They join returning veterans Elijah Pritchett at left tackle and Justin Evans at center, who alone brings 31 career starts into his senior season.

Experience, at Least on Paper

Combined, Nebraska's projected starting five brings more than 100 games of starting experience to the field — a sharp change from a unit that has often relied on undercooked, underexperienced pieces. Behind them, returners like Gunnar Gottula and Tyler Knaak provide depth, while four-star freshman Claude Mpouma is being developed for the future rather than thrown into the fire immediately.

Early Returns

546
Total yards of offense Nebraska piled up against Bowling Green, with Anthony Colandrea getting time to operate in the pocket without the pressure issues that plagued last season's offense.

Through two games, the results have been encouraging, if not conclusive. Of course, Ohio and Bowling Green aren't exactly murderer's row for a Big Ten offensive line.

The Real Exam Is Coming

The true test starts at Michigan State, then intensifies with Indiana and Oregon on back-to-back weekends in October — games where opposing defensive lines will be faster, stronger, and far better coached than anything Nebraska has faced so far.

The Gauntlet

  • Week 4 Michigan State First real physical test of the season
  • Week 6 Indiana Opens a brutal back-to-back stretch
  • Week 7 Oregon Among the best-coached fronts in the conference

If Wade's group can hold up physically against that level of competition, it would represent one of the most meaningful improvements of the Rhule era. If the same old issues resurface, it will be clear that better recruiting and coaching still haven't solved the fundamental problem up front.

Either way, the offensive line — long considered Nebraska's Achilles' heel — is finally getting the kind of investment and attention it has needed for years.
